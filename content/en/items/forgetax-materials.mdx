## What Are Forgetax Materials?

Tracking down forgetax materials is the part of progression that quietly decides whether your next upgrade happens today or next week. Whether you're chasing one stubborn rare component or stockpiling a full crafting set, forgetax materials sit between you and the build you actually want to run. The good news is that farming them is a solvable problem, not a lottery. Once you know which sources are reliable, which items are genuine bottlenecks, and which ones you can safely skip, the whole process turns from a grind into a checklist.

Forgetax materials are the components, fragments, and reagents that feed the forgetax crafting and upgrade path. In most versions of the game they aren't a single item — they're a family of items that only start mattering once you invest in the forgetax line. That's exactly why so many players hit a wall: they've been vendoring or ignoring materials for hours that suddenly become essential.

Treat the whole thing as an economy rather than a shopping list. Some materials are common enough to farm in bulk without a second thought. Others show up rarely, and those are the real bottlenecks that decide your pace.

**The four families of forgetax materials**

| Family | Typical role | Usual source | Rarity feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk commons | Filler for early upgrade steps | Open-world nodes, low-tier enemies | Very common |
| Refined intermediates | Crafted from commons | Crafting stations, vendors | Common–uncommon |
| Rare components | Gate mid and late upgrades | Bosses, timed events, deep runs | Uncommon–rare |
| Tokens / currency | Purchased from specialty vendors | Repeatable activities, dailies | Time-gated |

Exact names and recipes shift between patches, so confirm current values in-game before committing a long session. The framework below holds up regardless of which version you're on.

**How to tell which forgetax materials you actually need**

- Open the upgrade or crafting preview and read the requirement list from the top down.
- Flag every line item you currently hold zero of — those are your real targets.
- Ignore anything you already have a comfortable surplus of, even if it looks impressive.
- Screenshot the list. Requirement screens are far easier to read than your memory at 2 a.m.

## Where to Farm Forgetax Materials

Almost every forgetax material comes from one of five source types. The trick isn't finding a source — it's choosing sources that stack on top of each other so a single session feeds several needs at once.

| Source type | Reliability | Relative time per run | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open-world nodes | High | Low | Bulk commons | Respawn timers, competition |
| Standard enemies | Medium–high | Medium | Mixed commons plus occasional rares | Diminishing returns if you over-farm one camp |
| Boss encounters | Low–medium | High | Rare components | Heavy RNG; some runs yield nothing useful |
| Timed / rotating events | Medium | Medium | Event-exclusive materials | Windows close; missing one costs a full cycle |
| Vendors and exchange systems | Very high | Low | Converting surplus into what you lack | Exchange rates are usually unfavorable |

Player experience points to the same conclusion over and over: pure boss farming is the worst way to spend an evening. Bosses have the best per-kill ceiling, but the variance is punishing. The efficient approach is to stack a boss run with node routes on the way in and vendor conversions on the way out, so even a bad roll still moves you forward.

It's also worth checking whether your save still has one-time sources available — quest rewards, first-clear bonuses, and early handouts. These are essentially free forgetax materials, and they're easy to forget once you're deep into the grind and only thinking about repeatable loops.

For route ideas and patch-specific notes, the [community-written farming guides on Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/guides) are a good starting point. Player-authored guides frequently flag spawn quirks and rotation timing that official documentation skips entirely.

## Which Forgetax Materials Should You Prioritize?

Not every material deserves the same attention. Sorting your list into three tiers keeps you from burning a session on items that won't matter for weeks.

| Priority | What belongs here | Why it matters | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 — Bottlenecks | Rare components with only one or two sources | They block entire upgrade steps | Farm first, every session |
| Tier 2 — Volume materials | Commons and refined intermediates | Needed in large quantities; painful to gather late | Farm passively while chasing Tier 1 |
| Tier 3 — Speculative | Materials for builds you might try someday | Nice to have, rarely urgent | Bank a small buffer, then stop |

**Quick pre-session checklist**

- Do I know exactly which item is blocking my next upgrade?
- Does my route touch at least one Tier 1 source?
- Am I carrying a buffer of Tier 2 materials, or will I stall next week?
- Am I hoarding Tier 3 "just in case" while Tier 1 sits at zero?

Community reports often note that a material's perceived rarity is really a routing problem. Players farm inefficient loops, conclude an item is "impossible," and quit — when a shorter loop with better uptime would have solved it. Before you write off a drop, check whether your route is the actual problem.

## A Practical Forgetax Materials Farming Routine

A repeatable routine beats improvisation. Here's a structure that works whether you have thirty minutes or three hours.

1. **Identify the bottleneck.** Open the upgrade screen and name the single item holding you back. Write it down.
2. **Plan a loop, not a target.** Design a route that passes two or three source types instead of camping one spawn.
3. **Clear commons on the way.** Pick up volume materials during transit — they cost you seconds and save you hours later.
4. **Convert surplus.** Turn excess commons into refined materials at a station or vendor before your inventory caps out.
5. **Bank a buffer before spending.** Never spend your last rare component on an upgrade you're unsure about.
6. **Log what you got.** A one-line note per session prevents duplicate farming and makes progress visible.

| Phase | Goal | Materials targeted | Share of session |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | Confirm exact requirements | None | About 5% |
| Loop A | Clear Tier 1 bottlenecks | Rare components | 40–60% |
| Loop B | Passive volume gathering | Commons and intermediates | 30–40% |
| Wrap-up | Convert and bank | Refined materials | About 10% |

Consistency matters more than marathon sessions. Time-gated sources reward players who show up regularly, so short daily runs frequently outperform a single long weekend push. If your schedule is tight, protect the Tier 1 loop and let the rest happen incidentally.

## Common Mistakes That Waste Forgetax Materials

The most expensive mistake isn't a bad drop rate — it's impatience. Player experience repeatedly shows that spending rare forgetax materials on an interim upgrade feels great for an hour and costs a week of progress once the better option unlocks.

| Mistake | What it costs you | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Spending rares on a build you'll replace | Days of farming | Wait until you've committed to a direction |
| Over-farming one camp | Hours with no meaningful drops | Rotate two or three sources |
| Ignoring timed sources | Missed rotation cycles | Set a reminder for reset windows |
| Vendoring "junk" commons | Re-farming materials you already had | Check the upgrade list before selling anything |
| No inventory tracking | Duplicate farming | Keep a short written or screenshot log |

**Other habits worth breaking**

- Assuming a low drop rate means bad luck, when it usually means a bad route.
- Farming a material you already have a surplus of because it's easy and comfortable.
- Skipping the crafting preview and guessing at requirements.
- Letting your inventory fill up mid-run and losing pickups to a full bag.

Fix the top two items on that table and your effective farming rate improves immediately, without any change to your gear or skill.

## FAQ

**How many forgetax materials do I need in total?**
It depends entirely on the build you're pursuing and the current patch. The reliable method is to open the crafting preview for your target and add up the requirements listed there. Community-shared spreadsheets can help with planning, but the in-game preview is always the source of truth.

**What's the fastest way to farm forgetax materials?**
Stack sources instead of specializing. A route that combines node gathering, standard enemies, and a boss attempt gives you a baseline return even when the rare drop doesn't land. Pure boss farming has the highest ceiling and the worst average.

**Are forgetax materials tradable or bound to my account?**
This varies by item. Check the tags on each material in your inventory — bound items can't be traded or mailed, while others can move between players or accounts. Knowing which is which changes whether farming or trading is the smarter play.

**Should I farm forgetax materials before or after finishing the main story?**
In most cases, wait until you've unlocked the relevant vendors, stations, or difficulty tiers. Farming early often means gathering materials you can't yet refine and competing with weaker drop tables. Confirm what your current progression unlocks before you commit.

**What should I do if I'm stuck on a single rare material?**
Broaden your route before you broaden your playtime. Check community reports for alternate sources, verify that you're farming at the right tier, and make sure a timed source isn't the intended path. If all else fails, take a break from that target and farm volume materials — the bottleneck will still be there, and you'll be better stocked when you return.
