## Sword Smithing and Upgrades: Building a Blade Worth Keeping

Smithing is the foundation, and it rewards patience more than speed. A weapon that comes out of the forge with a solid base is worth upgrading; a mediocre one usually isn't, no matter how attached you are to it.

**Upgrade order that works for most players:**

- Pick one weapon per run as your "carry" and pour upgrades into it.
- Keep one cheap backup so you can still fill orders while the carry is being worked on.
- Upgrade base quality *before* you start rolling stats — stats multiply a base, they don't replace it.
- Stop upgrading a blade the moment its value stops justifying the cost.

That last point is the one players ignore most. An upgrade that costs more than the finished weapon will ever sell for is a donation, not an investment.

### Rolling Item Stats Without Wrecking Your Run

Stat rolls are the heart of the blacksmith roguelite genre, and *ForgeTax* leans into them hard. You're essentially gambling resources for a chance at better modifiers, and the house edge is real.

A practical way to sort whatever stats you roll:

| Stat Family | What It Usually Does | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Raw damage / quality | Raises the blade's headline number | High — always valuable |
| Speed / handling | Affects how the weapon performs in a fight | High if you plan to fight the dragon yourself |
| Durability | Keeps the weapon from degrading | Medium — situational but painful to ignore |
| Elemental or special effects | Adds conditional bonus damage or utility | Medium — great when it synergizes, dead weight when it doesn't |
| Value / trade modifiers | Improves what customers pay | Medium — pure economy, no combat help |

The takeaway: a roll that doesn't help you fight the dragon should be judged purely on whether it makes you money. If it does neither, it's noise.

### Chasing Great Successes

Great successes are the moment the run turns. They're rare, they're usually tied to timing or resource investment, and they're the reason players keep restarting after a wipe.

Three habits that help:

1. **Bank first, gamble second.** Never reroll with money you need for the tax.
2. **Commit when the base is high.** A great success on a strong blade is a run-winner; on a weak blade it's a waste.
3. **Know when to stop.** Chasing a perfect roll past your budget is the classic roguelite death spiral.

A forgetax guide can tell you the shape of the gamble, but the discipline is yours.

## The Dragon and the Forge Tax

Every run in *ForgeTax* bends toward the dragon. It demands its forge tax, and paying it is not optional — the question is whether you pay it comfortably, pay it painfully, or can't pay it at all.

The tax functions as a recurring checkpoint. Each time it comes around, it forces an honest accounting of how well you've actually been running your forge. If you've been spending every coin on rerolls, the tax will find you out.

**Dragon prep checklist:**

| Prep Area | Action | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| Finances | Set aside tax money before any big gamble | Removes the panic spiral |
| Weapon | Finish upgrades and stats on your carry first | The fight rewards one strong blade over three average ones |
| Backups | Keep a second usable weapon | Insurance if your main underperforms |
| Timing | Do your last upgrade *before* the tax check, not after | You'll know exactly what you're working with |
| Mindset | Treat a loss as data, not a verdict | Roguelites reward iteration |

Community reports suggest the dragon fight punishes under-prepared players far more than it punishes unlucky ones. That's encouraging news — it means preparation is the lever you actually control.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is ForgeTax a roguelike or a management game?**
It's a blacksmith roguelite with strong management elements. You run a forge, fill orders, and manage money, but runs are structured, failure is part of the loop, and the dragon fight gives each attempt a clear finish line. This forgetax guide treats it as a roguelite first because that's how its difficulty is designed.

**Do I need to pay the dragon's forge tax every run?**
The tax is a core pressure mechanic rather than an optional side objective. The exact cadence can change between patches, so check the current version in-game or on the store page rather than relying on older guides.

**Should I reroll stats or upgrade first?**
Upgrade first. Stats multiply the quality of a weapon's base, so rolling on a weak blade wastes resources even when you get a good result. Build the base, then gamble.

**How do I get great successes more often?**
Players report that great successes come from committing meaningful resources at the right moment rather than from spamming attempts. Practically: keep a healthy reserve, upgrade your best weapon, and accept that some runs simply won't produce one.

**What's the fastest way to improve at ForgeTax?**
Stop trying to win every run. Pick one thing to test per attempt — a spending pattern, a weapon path, a dragon prep routine — and adjust based on the result. That iterative habit is what separates a player who's read a forgetax guide from one who's actually getting better.
