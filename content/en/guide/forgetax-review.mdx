## The Core Loop: Smith, Roll, Upgrade, Repeat

Everything in ForgeTax feeds the same cycle, and understanding that cycle is the fastest way to stop losing runs. You gather materials, forge a blade, roll its stats, sell it, and reinvest the profit into better equipment. Then the dragon's deadline creeps closer and you do it all again, only faster and with better stock.

| Loop Stage | What You Do | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Gathering | Collect ore, ingots, and specialty materials | Better inputs raise the ceiling on everything |
| Forging | Shape a new sword at the anvil | Base quality determines how good the roll can be |
| Rolling | Reroll item stats to chase ideal combinations | This is where most of the power comes from |
| Selling | Move finished goods to buyers | Converts craft into the gold that funds upgrades |
| Upgrading | Improve tools, forge, and materials | Compounds across every future run |
| Paying | Settle the dragon's forge tax | The run's win condition and its timer |

### Forging and Upgrading Swords

The forge is the heart of the game. You choose a blade type, commit materials, and work the anvil. Timing and material quality matter, so a rushed sword made from cheap ore will not carry you far. Upgrading matters just as much: pushing a favorite sword to a higher tier can turn a mediocre roll into a genuine carry weapon.

Community reports suggest that new players under-invest in the forge itself and over-invest in single swords. That is a mistake. The forge is permanent; the sword is not.

### Rolling Item Stats and Chasing Great Successes

Stat rolling is the addictive part. Every finished piece lands somewhere on a spectrum, and the best outcomes — the great successes — are the ones you will remember. Damage, speed, and secondary effects all compete for space, so a "perfect" sword depends entirely on what you need for the current run.

| Roll Outcome | What It Means | How to React |
|---|---|---|
| Weak roll | Low stats, little upside | Sell it cheap and move on |
| Solid roll | Useful stats with no standout | Keep it as a workhorse blade |
| Strong roll | High stats in the right places | Upgrade it and build around it |
| Great success | Exceptional stats, often with bonus effects | This is your run-defining item |

The randomness is real, and it will occasionally frustrate you. But because runs are short, a bad roll costs you minutes rather than hours.

### Selling and Reinvesting

Gold is the connective tissue. Adventurers buy what you make, and the price scales with quality. The smart play is usually to sell the middle of your output and keep the exceptional pieces, then pour the proceeds into materials and forge upgrades. Players who hoard everything tend to stall right when the tax deadline arrives.

## The Dragon's Tax: How Runs Actually End

The dragon is not just a boss — it is the clock. The forge tax is the recurring payment that keeps your operation alive, and it escalates as your forge grows. That design choice gives every mundane decision weight: that extra reroll you wanted costs you time you may not have.

| Phase | Your Goal | What Failure Looks Like |
|---|---|---|
| Early run | Establish a reliable income of cheap swords | Running out of materials with nothing to sell |
| Mid run | Upgrade the forge and stockpile quality gear | Spending everything on rolls that do not land |
| Pre-deadline | Liquidate inventory and cover the tax | Coming up short and losing the run |
| Dragon confrontation | Fight or pay — depending on your build | Entering underprepared and losing everything |

You have options. Some players pay the tax and keep the forge running, building toward a stronger position. Others invest in combat capability and take the fight to the dragon directly. Both routes are viable, which is exactly why the game holds up across repeated attempts.

## Tips and Strategies for New Blacksmiths

A few habits separate players who clear the tax consistently from those who keep starting over.

| Tip | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| Upgrade the forge early | Permanent bonuses beat one-off good swords |
| Do not reroll mediocre items | Save rerolls for pieces with a high base quality |
| Keep one "carry" sword per run | A single strong weapon anchors your whole economy |
| Sell the middle of your inventory | Cash flow keeps materials coming |
| Watch the tax timer constantly | The clock is the real difficulty, not the dragon |
| Bank materials before the deadline | You cannot craft your way out with no ore |

## ForgeTax Review FAQ

**Is ForgeTax worth buying?**

If you enjoy crafting loops, upgrade economies, and short roguelite runs with a clear win condition, yes. This ForgeTax review found the dragon's tax mechanic to be the standout feature — it turns a cozy smithing game into something with real stakes.

**Is ForgeTax a roguelike or a roguelite?**

It is firmly a roguelite. Runs reset, but permanent forge upgrades and unlocks carry forward, so each attempt makes the next one a little more manageable.

**How long does a typical run take?**

Runs are designed to be short enough to finish in a single sitting, with progression that persists between attempts. That structure is a big part of why the game is easy to keep returning to.

**Is ForgeTax too hard for beginners?**

The opening hours are challenging, but the difficulty curve is mostly about learning the economy rather than raw reflexes. Community reports consistently say the game clicks once you stop hoarding and start reinvesting.

**What is the best early upgrade?**

Most experienced players prioritize forge and tool upgrades over individual swords. Those bonuses apply to every item you make from that point forward, which compounds quickly across runs.
