## How to Tell a Real Trailer From a Fake

Search results for any anticipated release fill up fast with reuploads, "concept trailers," AI-generated mockups, and reaction-bait edits. Knowing the difference saves you from building expectations on nothing.

| Signal | Genuine Official Upload | Fake or Fan-Made |
|---|---|---|
| Channel identity | Verified studio or publisher channel | Personal channel, reupload, or aggregator |
| Description text | Press contact, official links, legal line | Generic tags, no contact info |
| Upload timing | Announced in advance or tied to an event | Appears suddenly with no announcement |
| Asset quality | Consistent art direction and audio mix | Mismatched footage, stock music, inconsistent fonts |
| Cross-posting | Mirrored on other official platforms | Single-platform only |
| Comment section | Often moderated or limited | Filled with "is this real?" |

A quick rule: if the only place a trailer exists is one unverified channel, treat it as unconfirmed until an official source mirrors it. Community reports frequently flag convincing fakes within hours, but the safest habit is to wait for a second, independent official confirmation.

## Release Timeline: What Happens After the Trailer

| Stage | What It Means | What You Should Do |

| Official trailer | Release window established | Save the window, verify the source |

The spacing between these stages varies enormously between projects. Some releases move from trailer to launch in a matter of weeks; others sit in a marketing cycle for the better part of a year. The trailer's wording is your best clue about which pattern applies.

One practical tip: if a release window is given as a broad period rather than a fixed date, treat the later end of that period as the realistic expectation. Delays announced after a trailer are common across the industry, and planning for the outer edge of a window means you are rarely disappointed.
